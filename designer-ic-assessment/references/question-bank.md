# Question Bank
**Version:** 0.3
**Updated:** March 2026

## Structure

Five categories, 19 competencies. Each section contains designer and manager questions plus a Likert rating block. Behavioral questions always come first. The rating is asked at the end of each competency block, grounded in what was just said.

## Likert scale

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learner | Just getting started. Needs guidance to apply this. |
| 2 | Developing | Building awareness. Can do this with support but not yet independently. |
| 3 | Capable | Applies this independently and consistently. |
| 4 | Advanced | Goes beyond the basics. Others notice and benefit from their strength here. |
| 5 | Expert | Genuine mastery. Others come to them for this. Raising the bar for the team. |

## Interview guidance

Run the session as a natural conversation. Questions are guideposts, not a checklist. Follow the thread the person is giving you. Move between questions when it feels right, not when a number is checked off. Use follow-up probes when answers are thin. Don't probe more than twice per area before moving on.

## 1. Business Sense
**Category:** Knowledge
**What it covers:** Understanding how the business works, what success looks like commercially, and how design decisions connect to business outcomes.

### Designer

**Opening prompt:**
Tell me about a time a design decision you made was directly connected to a business outcome -- revenue, retention, conversion, cost. What was the connection?

*Probe if shallow:* How did you know the design was the lever? What would have happened if you'd made a different call?

**Follow thread:**
How do you typically stay connected to what the business is trying to achieve? Is that information coming to you, or are you going to get it?

*Probe if shallow:* Has your understanding of the business ever changed how you approached a design problem? What shifted?

**Follow thread:**
Tell me about a time you pushed back on a design request because you thought it was the wrong call for the business -- even if it was what a stakeholder wanted.

*Probe if shallow:* How did you make that case? Did it land?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 scale for business sense -- understanding how your design work connects to how the business actually works and what it's trying to achieve?
>
> 1 means you're still building that picture. 5 means you're regularly making design decisions with a clear view of the commercial implications and others come to you for that perspective."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
Can you point to a moment where this designer's understanding of the business -- or lack of it -- directly affected a design outcome?

*Probe if shallow:* Was it a positive or negative impact? Did they recognize the connection themselves?

**Follow thread:**
How does this designer stay connected to what the business is trying to achieve? Do they seek that context out, or do they wait for it to come to them?

*Probe if shallow:* Have they ever surprised you with business knowledge you didn't expect them to have?

**Follow thread:**
Has this designer ever pushed back on a request on business grounds -- not just design grounds? What happened?

*Probe if shallow:* Were they right? How did the team receive it?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 scale for business sense?
>
> 1 means they're still building a picture of how the business works. 5 means they regularly connect design decisions to commercial outcomes and others trust their read on the business."

## 2. Engineering Understanding
**Category:** Knowledge
**What it covers:** Working mental model of the technical environment, feasibility awareness, and designing for how things are actually built.

### Designer

**Opening prompt:**
Tell me about a design decision you made that required you to understand something about how the product was actually built. What did you need to learn and how did you learn it?

*Probe if shallow:* Did your understanding of the constraints change your design? How?

**Follow thread:**
How would the engineers you've worked with describe you as a collaborator? Be honest.

*Probe if shallow:* Is there something specific you've gotten better at? Something you're still working on?

**Follow thread:**
Have you designed for a product or feature that involved AI -- recommendations, predictions, generated content? What did you need to understand about how the system worked to design it well?

*Probe if shallow:* Did you design for what happens when it gets it wrong? How did you think about that?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 scale for engineering understanding?
>
> 1 means you rely heavily on engineers to tell you what's possible. 5 means you have a strong working mental model and engineers trust that your designs are buildable."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
How well does this designer understand the technical environment they design for? Do they have a working mental model, or do they need a lot of support from engineering to understand what's feasible?

*Probe if shallow:* Have you had to intervene because a design wasn't technically grounded?

**Follow thread:**
How would you describe this designer's relationship with the engineers they work with? Do engineers trust their work?

*Probe if shallow:* Have engineers ever come to you with concerns about buildability? Do they collaborate during implementation or hand off and disappear?

**Follow thread:**
If this designer has worked on AI-powered features, how did they approach understanding the underlying system? Did they design for edge cases and failure states?

*Probe if shallow:* Did they treat it as a black box or did they ask questions about how it behaves?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for engineering understanding?
>
> 1 means heavy reliance on engineering to define feasibility. 5 means strong technical mental model, builds well, engineers trust the work."

## 3. Domain Knowledge
**Category:** Knowledge
**What it covers:** Depth of understanding of the problem space, user context, and industry-specific nuance.

### Designer

**Opening prompt:**

*Probe if shallow:* How did you acquire that knowledge? Was it deliberate or did it accumulate over time?

**Follow thread:**
How do you stay current with what's happening in the space you design for? What's your actual routine?

*Probe if shallow:* Has something you learned recently changed how you think about your users? What shifted?

**Follow thread:**
Have you ever designed for a space where getting the domain wrong -- regulations, compliance, safety, legal -- could have caused real harm? How did you handle that?

*Probe if shallow:* Where did you go to verify you had it right? Did AI play any role, and how did you treat what it told you?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 for domain knowledge in the spaces you design for?
>
> 1 means you're still building basic familiarity. 5 means deep expertise -- you're the person others come to for context on the problem space."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
Has this designer ever made a call that required real domain knowledge -- something a designer from outside the space might have gotten wrong?

*Probe if shallow:* How did you know the call was right? Did they have to advocate for it, or did the team trust their read?

**Follow thread:**
Does this designer actively stay current with what's happening in the space they design for? How do you know?

*Probe if shallow:* Have they ever brought something new to the team from their own learning? Do they challenge assumptions about users or the market?

**Follow thread:**
Has this designer worked on anything where regulatory, compliance, or safety considerations were in play? How did they handle the responsibility?

*Probe if shallow:* Did they know to escalate or verify, or did they treat it like any other design problem? Did you have to intervene?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for domain knowledge?
>
> 1 means still building basic familiarity. 5 means deep expertise the team relies on."

## 4. Established UX Conventions
**Category:** Knowledge
**What it covers:** Understanding of interaction patterns, platform conventions, accessibility standards, and when to follow vs. break them intentionally.

### Designer

**Opening prompt:**
Tell me about a time you had to decide whether to follow an established UX convention or break from it. What was the call and how did you make it?

*Probe if shallow:* What would have been lost by following the convention? What would have been lost by breaking it?

**Follow thread:**
How do you keep your knowledge of platform conventions and accessibility standards current? What's your actual practice?

*Probe if shallow:* Has a convention or standard you learned recently changed how you approach something you'd been doing a certain way?

**Follow thread:**
Tell me about a time someone on your team or a stakeholder pushed for something that violated a UX convention in a way that would have hurt the user. What did you do?

*Probe if shallow:* How did you make the case? Did it change the outcome?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 for established UX conventions?
>
> 1 means you're still building awareness of the conventions that apply to your work. 5 means deep fluency -- you know the rules well enough to know exactly when and why to break them."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
How well does this designer understand and apply UX conventions, platform patterns, and accessibility standards? Can you give me an example either way?

*Probe if shallow:* Have you ever had to flag a convention violation in their work? How did they respond?

**Follow thread:**
Has this designer ever made a strong case for following -- or deliberately breaking -- a convention? What was the situation?

*Probe if shallow:* Were they right? How did the team receive their reasoning?

**Follow thread:**
How does this designer handle situations where a stakeholder or teammate pushes for something that would hurt the user experience based on convention or accessibility?

*Probe if shallow:* Do they defer, or do they advocate? Can they back it up with reasoning?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for established UX conventions?
>
> 1 means still building awareness. 5 means deep fluency, knows when and why to follow or break conventions, and can make the case clearly."

## 5. Systems Thinking
**Category:** Craft
**What it covers:** Ability to see patterns, design at scale, create reusable solutions, and understand how components interact across a product.

### Designer

**Opening prompt:**
Tell me about a time you identified a pattern in a product that was creating problems at scale -- inconsistency, duplication, fragility. What did you do about it?

*Probe if shallow:* How did you identify it? Was it obvious to others or did you have to make the case?

**Follow thread:**
How do you approach designing something new when you know it needs to work within a larger system? Walk me through how that thinking shows up in your process.

*Probe if shallow:* What breaks when designers don't think this way? Have you seen that happen?

**Follow thread:**
Tell me about a contribution you've made to a design system -- a pattern, a component, a principle. What problem were you solving and what made the solution durable?

*Probe if shallow:* Has it held up? Has it been adopted? Has anything broken it?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 for systems thinking?
>
> 1 means you're primarily focused on individual screens or flows. 5 means you're designing for scale and coherence across the whole product, and others rely on your systems thinking to keep things from fragmenting."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
Can you give me an example of this designer thinking at a systems level -- seeing patterns, designing for reuse, or identifying something that was fragile at scale?

*Probe if shallow:* Did they see it themselves or did someone point it out? What did they do with it?

**Follow thread:**
How does this designer approach new work in the context of the broader design system? Do they design for fit, or do they design in isolation and figure out the fit later?

*Probe if shallow:* Have you had to pull back a design because it wasn't considering the system?

**Follow thread:**
What has this designer contributed to your design system? Was it adopted? Has it held up?

*Probe if shallow:* How do other designers engage with their contributions?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for systems thinking?
>
> 1 means focused on individual screens. 5 means designing for coherence and scale, others rely on them to keep the system together."

**Follow thread:**

*Probe if shallow:* Did they get it right? Did it hold up?

**Follow thread:**

*Probe if shallow:* Have you ever had to flag a research quality concern to them? How did they respond?

*Probe if shallow:* What did you try first? What didn't work? How did you know when you had it right?

**Follow thread:**
How do you approach edge cases and failure states in your work? Are you designing for them from the start or discovering them later?

*Probe if shallow:* Tell me about a time an edge case you hadn't anticipated caused a real problem. What happened?

**Follow thread:**
Tell me about a piece of interaction design work you'd do differently now. What would you change and why?

*Probe if shallow:* What did you know then? What do you know now that you didn't?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 for interaction design?
>
> 1 means you're still developing fluency with interaction patterns and edge case thinking. 5 means exceptional depth -- others come to you to pressure test their flows and your interaction thinking raises the bar for the team."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
How would you describe the quality of this designer's interaction thinking? Not the visual output -- the decisions underneath it. Edge cases, flows, error states.

*Probe if shallow:* Can you point to a piece of their work that shows their interaction design at its best?

**Follow thread:**
Has this designer ever caught an edge case or failure state that others missed? What happened?

*Probe if shallow:* Do they tend to find these themselves or do they surface in QA or user testing?

**Follow thread:**
Where do you think this designer still has room to grow in their interaction design? Be honest.

*Probe if shallow:* Have you had to send work back because the interaction thinking wasn't thorough enough?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for interaction design?
>
> 1 means developing fluency. 5 means exceptional depth in interaction thinking, edge cases, and flow design -- raises the bar for the team."

## 10. Prototyping & Motion Design
**Category:** Craft
**What it covers:** Ability to prototype ideas at the right fidelity, use motion to communicate interaction, and use prototyping as a thinking and validation tool.

### Designer

**Opening prompt:**
Tell me about a time prototyping changed what you thought was the right design. What did you learn from building it that you wouldn't have learned from a static mock?

*Probe if shallow:* At what point in your process do you typically prototype? Has that changed over time?

**Follow thread:**
How do you decide what fidelity to prototype at? What factors drive that call?

*Probe if shallow:* Tell me about a time you over- or under-prototyped. What happened?

**Follow thread:**
How do you use motion and animation in your work? Is it intentional communication or more decorative? Tell me about a specific example.

*Probe if shallow:* How did you decide what to animate? How did you know it was helping rather than adding noise?

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 for prototyping and motion design?
>
> 1 means prototyping is still a developing skill and motion is rarely part of your work. 5 means you use prototyping fluidly as a thinking tool and motion is an intentional, well-considered part of how you communicate interaction."

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
How does this designer use prototyping in their process? Is it a genuine thinking tool or does it come late as a demonstration?

*Probe if shallow:* Can you think of a time prototyping changed the design? Did they initiate it or did someone suggest it?

**Follow thread:**
Does this designer make deliberate choices about prototype fidelity, or do they tend to default to one approach?

*Probe if shallow:* Have you ever felt they spent too much or too little time prototyping?

**Follow thread:**
How does motion and animation show up in their work? Is it intentional or decorative?

*Probe if shallow:* Has motion ever been a point of feedback -- positively or negatively -- in their work?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for prototyping and motion design?
>
> 1 means prototyping is still developing and motion is rarely considered. 5 means fluent use of prototyping as a thinking tool and intentional, well-calibrated motion design."

**Follow thread:**
How do you use AI to accelerate your own learning? Tell me about a specific case.

*Probe if shallow:* How did you verify what it told you? Did you find errors or gaps?

**Follow thread:**

### Designer rating
> "Based on what you've shared, where would you put yourself on a 1 to 5 for ability to learn quickly?

### Manager

**Opening prompt:**
Think about a time this designer had to learn something new quickly -- a new domain, tool, or type of problem. How did they approach it and how accurate was their learning?

*Probe if shallow:* Did gaps surface later? Did you have to correct any misunderstandings?

**Follow thread:**
How does this designer respond when they're wrong about something? Have you seen that happen?

*Probe if shallow:* Did they own it quickly or was it more of a slow acknowledgment? Did it change their behavior?

**Follow thread:**
Have you seen evidence of this designer updating their thinking based on new information? Something they used to do or believe that's clearly shifted?

*Probe if shallow:* Did they make that change themselves or did someone have to push them?

### Manager rating
> "Based on what you've observed, where would you put this designer on a 1 to 5 for ability to learn quickly?
>
> 1 means slow to pick up new things or tends to stay in familiar territory. 5 means fast, accurate learner who visibly integrates new knowledge into their work."

# MANAGER BREADTH & DEPTH ASSESSMENT

These questions are asked during the manager session only, in two places:

1. **During setup** -- before any competency questions, to establish the manager's independent breadth read
2. **At the end of the session** -- to surface depth vs. surface-level coverage across categories

---

## During setup: Breadth scope

Ask after weights are confirmed, before any competency questions begin.

*Prompt:*
> "Before we dig into specific competencies, I want to get your read on scope. Looking across all five categories -- Knowledge, Craft, Behaviors, Communication and Collaboration, and AI Fluency -- which competency areas do you consider actively in play for this designer right now, this cycle? And which ones are dormant or genuinely outside their current role?"

*Agent guidance:*
Go through each category together. For each one, ask the manager to call out which specific competencies within it are active vs. not in scope. Record this as the manager's breadth read -- it will be compared against the designer's own scope flags in the reveal meeting.

If the manager is unsure about a competency, note it as "unclear" rather than forcing a call.

*Why this matters:*
The manager may see breadth the designer doesn't claim (e.g. the designer undersells their mentoring impact). Or the manager may have a narrower view of scope than the designer does. Either gap is worth surfacing.

---

## End of session: Depth vs. breadth question

Ask this after all competency questions are complete, before generating the summary.

*Prompt:*
> "Stepping back across everything you've shared -- where do you see genuine depth in this designer's work? Places where they've really gone beyond the surface and built real mastery? And where do you think they're covering the ground but haven't gone deep yet?"

*Agent follow-ups if shallow:*
- Can you point to specific competencies or projects where the depth is most visible?
- Is there a category where you feel like they're spread thin -- active across it but not really owning any of it at a high level?

*Level signal:*
Depth at principal level should be visible across multiple categories, not just one. A principal who is deep in Craft but surface-level in Communication and AI Fluency is a different profile from one who has built real depth across all five. The manager's answer here feeds directly into the breadth section of the summary and the conversation starters for the reveal.

---

## Breadth comparison in the reveal meeting

The FigJam board should include a **breadth comparison row** in Zone 1 -- Score Overview. It shows:

| | Designer's active scope | Manager's active scope |
|---|---|---|
| Competencies in active scope | [X] of 19 | [Y] of 19 |
| Scope agreement | [list competencies both called active] | |
| Scope gap | [competencies where views differ] | |

A scope gap -- where the designer claims a competency is active but the manager doesn't, or vice versa -- is one of the most interesting conversation starters in the reveal meeting. It often reveals either visibility gaps on the manager's side, or underselling on the designer's side.
