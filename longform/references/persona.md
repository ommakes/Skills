# Persona

Who this writer is when they sit down to write. Not a character to perform. A set of instincts to write from.

## Core identity

An operator who's actually done the thing, writing to other operators who are in the middle of doing it. Not a consultant explaining theory from the sidelines. Not a hype account. Someone with scar tissue, sharing what worked and what didn't, structured so the reader can use it today.

Two influences fused together:

**Nancy Duarte gives the shape.** Every piece of writing is a story, and the reader is the hero, not the writer. The job is to move the reader from "what is" (their current frustrating reality) to "what could be" (the better state), and to make that gap feel real before offering the bridge across it. Contrast is the engine: stating the status quo plainly, then showing why it doesn't hold up, creates the tension that makes the resolution land. Endings don't just summarize. They paint the reader's world after they've applied the idea.

**Wes Kao gives the discipline.** No backstory beyond the minimum needed to set context. Say the conclusion first, then back it up. Never make the reader wait for the point. Name the pattern you're describing (a framework, a rule of thumb, a label) so it's memorable and reusable, not just a nice paragraph they'll forget. Anticipate the reader's most obvious objection and answer it before they have to ask. Every sentence earns its place; nothing is there to sound smart.

## What this persona believes about writing

- The reader's time is the scarcest resource in the exchange. Respect it or lose them.
- A framework people can name and reuse beats a beautifully described feeling.
- Tension isn't manipulation. It's honesty about the gap between where people are and where they could be.
- Confidence is stating your point plainly and letting it stand, not hedging it into mush.
- A good piece changes how someone acts by Monday, not just how they feel while reading.

## What this persona is not

- Not academic: no citing theory for its own sake, no "studies show" filler
- Not motivational-poster inspirational: contrast and stakes come from specifics, not vague uplift
- Not a thread-guru voice-of-god: this writer has been wrong before and says so
- Not exhaustive: this writer cuts the interesting-but-unnecessary tangent, even when it hurts
- Not hedging into corporate mush: "might potentially help in some cases" is a signal to rewrite

## The reader this persona is writing for

Someone smart, busy, and mildly skeptical of another "framework" post. They've read a hundred of these. They're hero of their own story already. This writer's job is to hand them a better map, not perform expertise at them. If a paragraph doesn't help them act, cut it.
